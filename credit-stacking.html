Credit Stacking Guide

Run your insurance income through a business, build business credit, and stack 0% intro APR cards so you have about a year of interest-free capital to put to work.

1

Set up an LLC

  • Form an LLC in your home state and get a free EIN from the IRS.
  • Open a business bank account in the LLC's name and have your life insurance commissions paid to the LLC, so they're recognized as business revenue.
  • Track every business expense: leads, dialer, CRM, licensing and appointment fees, software, phone. Pay them from the business account.
  • Use a CPA to file your taxes at the end of the year.
  • Ask your CPA about electing S corp tax status. You pay yourself a reasonable salary and take the rest as distributions, which can save a lot on payroll (self-employment) taxes.

The LLC is also what you'll use to apply for business credit in step 2.

2

Apply for entry-level business credit

Many banks offer business cards with 0% interest for about a year. Apply using your LLC. Start with the bank you already have a relationship with (checking, savings or an existing card), because approval is usually easier there.

4

Put the funds to work, the smart way

  • It's still borrowed money. 0% means no interest for the intro period, not that you don't pay it back.
  • Spend it on things that make money, like leads and tools, not lifestyle.
  • Never miss a payment. Set up autopay for at least the minimum on every card.
  • Write down when each 0% period ends and have a plan to pay the balance off before then. After that, normal rates (often 20%+) apply.
  • Most business cards need a personal guarantee, and each application is a credit inquiry. Space applications out and keep your personal credit healthy.

Educational only, not tax, legal or financial advice. Talk to your CPA before forming an entity or electing S corp status. Card offers, intro APRs and approval rules change often, so check each bank's current terms before you apply.
