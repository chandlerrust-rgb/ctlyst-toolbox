Objections and Word Tracks

An objection usually means "I'm not sure yet," not "no." Acknowledge it, ask a question to find the real concern, then answer that. Never argue, and never pressure.

The pattern for every objection

  1. Acknowledge: "That makes total sense."
  2. Clarify: ask one question to find what's really behind it.
  3. Answer the real concern, briefly.
  4. Check: "Does that help?" Then go back to where you were.

Common objections

"I need to think about it."

"Totally fair. Usually when someone says that, there's one part they're unsure about. Is it the coverage amount, the monthly cost, or something else?" Answer that one thing.

"It's too expensive."

"I hear you. Is it the price itself, or is it more than you planned to spend each month?" Then adjust the coverage to fit a number they're comfortable with. Don't drop the coverage below what actually solves their problem without saying so.

"I need to talk to my spouse."

"Of course. This affects them most. What do you think they'll ask?" Answer those questions now, then offer a three-way call or a set time to talk with both of them.

"I already have coverage through work."

"That's great. Do you know how much it is, and what happens to it if you change jobs or retire?" Most work coverage is 1–2× salary and ends when the job does.

"Just send me something in the mail."

"Happy to. So I send the right thing, can I ask two quick questions?" Go back to the fact-find. If they still want it mailed, book a time to go over it together.

"How did you get my information?"

Be specific and honest: when and where they requested information. If they don't want to be contacted, thank them and mark them do-not-call right away.

"I'm not interested."

"No problem. Can I ask what made you look into it in the first place?" If the answer is still no, respect it and end the call politely.

"I want to shop around."

"Smart. I work with several carriers, so shopping is what I just did for you. What would you be looking for that we haven't covered?"

Stay compliant

Never misrepresent a product, promise approval, or pressure someone into a policy they can't afford. Respect do-not-call requests immediately. Follow your carriers' and state's rules on replacements and disclosures.
