Present

Before numbers

Now before we go over the packages, I’m going to explain how this works. It’s not like the grocery store where you see it, like it, buy it. We have to get approved. The carriers look at what’s called the MIB report, the Medical Information Bureau. It’s a compilation of your medical records, hospitalizations and prescriptions over the last few years.

The final decision is up to the carrier, which is why we spent a little time on your health.

Benefits · grab a pen and paper

I’m going to give you some information about the benefits that come with this type of plan. First, write down my name, phone number and NPN (my national producer number, my license ID).

  • Immediate coverage: covered from your first premium, no 2-year waiting period.Only on level / day-1 plans. Not on graded or guaranteed issue.
  • Locked in: the premium never increases and the coverage never decreases.
  • Tax-free: the death benefit is generally paid tax-free.
  • Living benefit: with a terminal illness diagnosis, you can access part of the benefit while you’re still living.
  • Cash value: the policy builds cash value over time.
  • Accidental benefit: on many plans, an accidental death pays extra.
  • Permanent coverage: it’s whole life, so it never expires.

Based on what you’ve told me, the system has built 3 packages, and you can decide which makes the most sense. We can always adjust. The goal is the right amount of coverage at the best price, but at the end of the day it has to be affordable. Does that make sense? Go ahead and write down Bronze, Silver and Gold.

Bronze

The Bronze option covers a basic funeral, as long as it’s not on the fancy end, but won’t leave money behind for (beneficiary). That’s (coverage) for ($) a month.

Silver

The Silver option, which most people lean toward, covers the full cost plus future inflation, so (beneficiary) isn’t burdened by any of the expense. That’s (coverage) for ($) a month.

Gold

The Gold option covers the full cost of the funeral and leaves some money behind for (beneficiary). That’s (coverage) for ($) a month.

Golden question

Given those three options, Gold, Silver and Bronze, which one makes the most sense for you?

Objections

Intro objections

“I’m looking for benefits!”

Perfect. These are private final expense and life plans that a lot of veterans use alongside their VA benefits, and some carriers offer veteran-friendly rates. Do you currently have anything in place?

Then go back to the script.

“The VA will take care of my burial.”

I completely understand. That’s a common misconception. The VA can provide burial in a national cemetery and, for some veterans, a partial burial allowance, but it usually doesn’t cover the full cost of a funeral or cremation. So God forbid something happened today, who would be responsible for the rest of the funeral expenses and picking up the pieces tomorrow?

VA burial benefits (va.gov) →

Then go back to the script.

“I don’t remember filling this out.”

Perfect, I usually don’t remember what I ate for breakfast most days. You put your date of birth as ___, correct? Most people’s main concern when they fill this out is making sure their final expense doesn’t fall as a burden on their loved ones, or leaving some extra money behind. Do you currently have anything in place?

Then go back to the script.

“I’m not interested.”

I completely understand. When you sent this request in, what was your main concern? Do you have any coverage in place to pay for your burial or cremation?

If yes: Perfect. When you sent this in, were you trying to save some money or add more coverage?

Then go back to the script.

“This isn’t a good time / I’m busy.”

Perfect, we’re really backed up too. I’ll make this quick. You put your date of birth as ___, correct?

Only if they’re driving or at work: don’t continue. Ask for the best time to reach them, and whether they were focused on burial or cremation, before ending the call.

“I already have coverage.”

Perfect, that makes my job a lot easier. Did you get that through the VA or private life insurance?

Through the VA: So you have the VGLI term policy that goes up in price over time, correct? So when you sent this in, you were looking for something more permanent that’s guaranteed to pay out to your loved ones?

Private insurance: How much are you covered for, and how much are you paying? Then either beat the price or add coverage.

  • Over $20K and you can’t beat the price: So that coverage is to leave a legacy, and you were looking to cover your funeral expenses so they don’t fall on your loved ones, correct?
  • Under $20K and you can’t beat the price: So that coverage is for your funeral expenses, and you were looking to leave a legacy behind, correct?
  • You can beat the price: ask a few quick health questions (heart attack, stroke, cancer), quick-quote, and show them the savings or the added coverage.
  • Colonial Penn / Globe Life / American Income: these often have 2–5 year waiting periods. If it was recent: were you trying to save some money? If they’ve had it a while: since that takes care of the funeral, were you looking to leave a legacy behind?

Numbers objections

“Can you send it in the mail?”

I completely understand, but this isn’t something where you see it, like it and buy it. We have to submit the request for coverage and hope for the approval. So given these three options, which one makes the most sense for you: Gold, Silver or Bronze?

“I need to think about it.”

I completely understand. Did you want to think about whether you need the coverage at all, or which amount to go with?

Which amount: Do you mind if I make a recommendation? Let’s start with the bare minimum, make sure your loved ones are protected today and that it’s comfortable and affordable. We can always increase it later. Does that make sense? Jump to the app.

Whether they need it: spin back to the why. Any insurance or savings? Is it enough? ($10K usually isn’t enough for a burial; most add $5K for inflation.) “Do you mind if I make a recommendation?”

“I need to talk to my wife.”

Push back 1: I completely understand. She’s the beneficiary, so she should know what’s going on. Do you need to talk to her about which one to go with, or whether to do this at all? Because you said this was to protect her, correct?

Push back 2: Do you mind if I make a recommendation? Let’s start with the bare minimum, get the approval and your foot in the door, and make sure it’s comfortable. We can always increase it later. Jump to the app.

Social Security objections

Hesitant to give the social

Push back 1: I completely understand. The main reason they ask is that it’s the only way the carrier can look you up in the Medical Information Bureau, and it’s what’s on the death certificate so your loved ones get paid out. Does that make sense? Go ahead with your social.

Push back 2: No worries. Go ahead and confirm the first 5 and I can validate the full number for you.

Push back 3: Do you get text messages on this phone? I can show you better than I can tell you. (Text a picture of the application.) Do you see the carrier name and your name? Perfect, verify your social when you’re ready.

Banking objections

Hesitant to give the account number

I completely understand. Quick question: have you ever written or received a check? At the bottom of every check is the routing and account number, because that information can’t be used to buy something online or go on a shopping spree. What would be weird is if I asked for a debit or credit card right away. Does that make sense? Go ahead with your account number.

“Why do you need my banking now?”

I completely understand. The carrier needs a payment method on file to put the policy in force once it’s approved, and it has to be in your name. Do you get text messages on this phone? I’ll send you a picture of my screen so you can see exactly what we’re filling out. (Text a picture of the application with the carrier name and their name.) Do you see your name and the carrier there? Go ahead when you’re ready.
